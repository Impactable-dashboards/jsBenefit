THE STRATEGIC THESIS
Jennifer already has what most firms spend years trying to build: a Forbes article, an active LinkedIn presence, a podcast co-host seat, YouTube content, 32 years of expertise, and proof that LinkedIn visibility directly influenced a recent 50-employee group win. What is missing is not more content. It is the deliberate system that converts all of that visibility into booked appointments — a defined audience, a specific conversion offer, paid distribution behind the best assets, retargeting that follows warm prospects, and a clear path to Jennifer’s calendar. That system does not exist yet. Building it is the entire job.

Five gaps nobody owns

GAP 01 · OPPORTUNITY: HIGH
Named-Outcome Proof at Scale
No competitor publishes quantified, named-strategy case studies with specific dollar outcomes. JSBG: 5. OneDigital: 1 (percentage only). Precision Benefits: 0. Exude: 0. Triton: 0. This is not a future advantage to build. It exists today. The gap is amplification, not production.
Evidence: All four competitor sites researched. Dollar-figure case study count: JSBG = 5 / All others = 0. The case studies are on the website but are not being served to HR Directors and CFOs before they renew with someone else.
GAP 02 · OPPORTUNITY: HIGH
Founder Access as a Named Guarantee, Not a Brand Attribute
Every firm claims personal service. No competitor ties named-individual accountability — with specific credentials and carrier relationships — directly to stated client outcomes. OneDigital markets a platform, not a person. Precision names an advisor in testimonials but does not center the founder. Exude markets team culture. Triton is entirely anonymous.
Why it matters: A CFO signing a benefits contract worth $200K–$500K/year is not buying a firm. They are buying a person's judgment. The primary fear in broker relationships is that the person who sold the account becomes unavailable after signing. JSBG is the only firm where the founder is explicitly the delivery mechanism.
GAP 03 · OPPORTUNITY: MEDIUM-HIGH
Captive Positioned as Earned Access, Not a Menu Checkbox
Every competitor lists captive as a service line bullet. No one positions the expertise as candidate-specific, earned over 15 years, with a published $380K outcome. An employer with 200+ employees and favorable claims history entering a captive conversation is worth 3–5x the annual retainer of a standard group health renewal. The conversation is uncontested.
Evidence: OneDigital has a Captive Center of Excellence but promotes it as a platform feature. No competitor has a captive case study with named industry, employee count, and dollar figure. JSBG has one.
GAP 04 · OPPORTUNITY: HIGH
Talent and Retention Framing for the CEO Buyer
All five firms lead with cost reduction. None prominently position benefits strategy as a talent acquisition and retention differentiator — despite this being the primary reason a CEO (not a CFO) chooses to invest in better benefits. The HSA case study (benefits moved from last to first in employee satisfaction) is the proof story. The message lane is uncontested.
Target: CEOs at 50–200 employee professional services, technology, and healthcare-staffing firms actively competing for talent against larger employers. Distinct buyer, distinct message, distinct case study.
GAP 05 · OPPORTUNITY: MEDIUM
Proactive Pre-Renewal Intervention as a Marketing Message
No competitor markets proactive renewal monitoring. All positioning is reactive: "call us when you get your number." Case Study 5 demonstrates exactly this capability (18% to 4.2%, three phone calls). The more powerful message is forward-looking: "we monitor your renewal cycle and intervene before you see the number." The 90–180 day pre-renewal window is the highest-value marketing moment in this category.
Campaign timing: August–October annually for calendar-year group health plans. No enrichment tool required. Run heavier budget in this window every year.
